# Design assistant

Part of the [garden design app](../garden-design-app/intent-brief.md). This document owns how the app turns what a person wants into garden designs. See the [intent index](../README.md) for the other parts.

## My read

The app does the heavy design work, so nobody faces a blank canvas. People come with very different knowledge. A beginner does not know what they want, so the app shows several different ideas and learns from their reactions. An experienced gardener knows their favorite plants and has opinions, so the app treats those as rules and designs around them.

A design often starts from a feeling, not a plant. "A peaceful, private place to sit after work" is a real starting point. The app turns it into plants, areas, and a layout on the real property.

## What matters most

- **Start anywhere.** A feeling, a color, wildlife, safety, a favorite plant, a reference photo, or a review of the yard as it is.
- **Real choices.** Designs differ from each other in a meaningful way.
- **The person stays in control.** Every plant the AI places can be moved, removed, or replaced.

## The experience you appear to want

You say what you want. Starting points:
- a feeling, such as peaceful, private, or a place to sit;
- a color theme;
- wildlife, such as songbirds, hummingbirds, or butterflies;
- safety, such as no thorns or toxic plants near kids and pets;
- favorite plants to design around;
- a photo of a garden you like: "use the style of this garden";
- a review of your yard now: what works, what does not, and which spots are empty.

The app shows 3 to 5 designs on your real plan. You pick one and say what you like and do not like. You lock plants you love. You select an area and ask the app to rework it, or select a plant and ask for a replacement.

## Boundaries

### Must be true

- Designs respect the sun class of each area, plant spacing, and the existing permanent features.
- Locked plants stay in every new design and rework.
- Ideas from a reference photo are labeled as borrowed, and the app uses Indiana plants with a similar look.
- The AI picks plants, areas, and reasons. Code does spacing, sun checks, and plant facts.

### Must be avoided

- A blank-canvas tool that makes the person do the design work.
- Designs that differ only in small details.
- Plants placed where they cannot grow.

## What seems settled

- Beginners pick from 3 to 5 options. Experienced users give constraints.
- The review of the current yard is honest about what does not look good.

## Possibilities, not decisions

- Budget and the amount of change the person accepts, from the first memo.
- Paths, stonework, and fence materials in designs.
