# Garden plan

Part of the [garden design app](../garden-design-app/intent-brief.md). This document owns what a person takes away: the steps from the yard they have to the garden they want. See the [intent index](../README.md) for the other parts.

## My read

A beautiful design is only half of the value. The person must also know what to do next. Gardening runs on time: seeds and bulbs go in months before they show, some plants must come out every fall, and trees take years. The plan turns a design into steps on a calendar that a first-time gardener can follow.

## What matters most

- **When to do each thing.** Planting windows and care tasks on a calendar.
- **What to buy.** Plants, counts, and the effort each one needs.
- **No surprises.** Yearly work, such as lifting dahlias, is visible before you commit.

## The experience you appear to want

After you choose and edit a design, you open your plan. It shows:
- a shopping list: each plant, how many, annual or perennial, and its effort;
- a calendar: when to plant each item so it shows up at the right time, and when to do each care task through the year;
- for each plant: the soil, the sun, and how long it takes to grow;
- notes on what is popular and easy to grow.

The plan follows the design. When you change the design, the plan changes too.

## Boundaries

### Must be true

- The plan comes from the design and the plant facts. It is never written by hand.
- It covers planting windows, care by month, time to grow, sun, soil, and replanting work.
- It uses Indiana timing.

### Must be avoided

- Advice that is not tied to the person's own design.
- Plant facts that the AI makes up.

## What seems settled

- A step-by-step plan from the yard now to the goal is a core output.

## Possibilities, not decisions

- Costs for each item, once budget is in scope.
- A plan in phases over several years, for large changes such as new trees.
- Reminders when a task is due.
